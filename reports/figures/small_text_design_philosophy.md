# Evidentiary Plate

Evidentiary Plate treats a scientific comparison as a physical artifact: ruled
lines, measured intervals, restrained annotations, and source material presented
without cosmetic disguise. Space is structural rather than decorative. Every
alignment should feel meticulously calibrated, as if the page were assembled by
an expert through countless rounds of optical correction.

The palette is drawn from archival paper, carbon ink, and a single diagnostic
red. Color never decorates; it marks a failure, a discontinuity, or an inference
that deserves attention. Native evidence remains quiet and neutral, while the
few red interventions carry the force of painstaking editorial judgment.

Scale communicates the argument. The input images occupy enough space for the
reader to inspect the lost detail directly, while outputs are arranged as
parallel specimens under identical rules. Repetition creates rhythm and makes
deviation visible. The composition must look labored over with master-level care,
with no ornamental softness and no element placed approximately.

Typography behaves like an instrument panel: a serif voice establishes the
claim, a monospaced voice preserves model output, and compact sans-serif labels
identify conditions. Text is sparse and evidentiary, never explanatory prose.
Precise baselines, consistent measures, and generous margins make the result feel
like the product of deep expertise rather than an automated graphic.

The finished plate should reward both immediate and close reading. At a glance,
the eye sees a sharp source, a degraded source, and three divergent outcomes. On
inspection, exact strings and aggregate measurements prove the claim. Every
detail must display painstaking craftsmanship while allowing the evidence—not
the styling—to remain the dominant visual fact.
